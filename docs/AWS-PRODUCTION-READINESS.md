## Implemented local control and migration boundary

The service derives the submitter from the server-written `REQUEST_SUBMITTED` event, not a client-supplied field. The decision path checks provenance before looking up a replay receipt. The existing single-decision lifecycle has at most two ordinary events; a future longer history/archival design must preserve provenance independently of the 50-event read bound. Existing records missing valid provenance will return 503 until investigated; no automatic backfill or bypass is provided. DynamoDB key/data layout is unchanged.

This is **same-principal decision exclusion**. Different assumed-role sessions or multiple accounts belonging to one human can have different IDs. It does not prove separate humans, effective least privilege or full production segregation of duties. The earlier deployed demo allowed one owner to exercise all roles; its evidence remains historical and does not prove the new controls.

Local HTTP uses two distinct random environment tokens mapped to separate synthetic requester/approver IDs. They are development credentials, not an IAM or production identity solution. The requester token cannot decide; the domain policy also rejects a dual-role principal's own decision. No keys or tokens are written into evidence.

## Failure and recovery playbook

1. **503 on a mutation:** the result may be uncertain. A provider can commit before its acknowledgement is lost. Retry with the same authenticated identity, URL, idempotency key and unchanged body, with bounded backoff. Do not generate a new key or assert that no write happened. Reread request/audit to reconcile; escalate persistent failures.
2. **Submission audit unavailable:** stop decisions for that record. Inspect preserved request/event data through authorised operational access. Do not invent an actor, edit raw evidence or disable the control. Reconcile with the service owner before an explicitly reviewed repair.
3. **409:** reread current state and distinguish a stale version, changed-key payload conflict or duplicate ID. Do not blindly resubmit a different business decision.
4. **403:** verify intended principal/role mapping; a submitter must use a separately authorised approver workflow. Never grant an anonymous route or broader runtime role to bypass a failure.
5. **Release regression:** stop promotion, retain the failing artefact/outputs and follow the reviewed rollback plan. A security-control rollback needs explicit risk review; no automatic deployment or destructive data migration is included here.
